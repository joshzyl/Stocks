## 1. Headline verdict

1. **The best risk-adjusted deployments right now are the higher-quality watchlist names and the SGD carry — not QFIN and not most of the "deployment candidates."** Specifically: **Tencent** and **JD.com** (buy-on-weakness quality/value), the **CapitaLand Ascott Trust / Genting SGD carry**, and **China Unicom** (improving-FCF income) screen ahead of QFIN, CR Medical, Haidilao, SMPL and PayPal on a permanent-loss-adjusted basis.
2. **"Keep firepower and wait" is broadly the right instinct — but not for everything.** BABA, Baidu, PayPal, Flutter and the two China banks are genuinely "wait for a better price / a confirming catalyst." But **Tencent and JD are already at levels worth starting**, and the **carry trade is a positive-carry way to be paid while you wait.**
3. **Genting: trim discipline, not add.** You already hold a large winning position. The balance sheet (net cash, sub-book) makes it the lowest-permanent-loss carry base, but the dividend is not covered by earnings and the RWS 2.0 payoff is 2028–2030. Take it to your ~10% cap on strength; keep it, don't grow it beyond the cap.
4. **QFIN is a high-variance *special situation*, not a core holding.** It has the highest absolute upside among the equities if the accounts are clean, but a bimodal outcome (VIE/ADR + still-deteriorating credit). The smart-money ownership and extreme valuation justify a *sized-for-ruin* position, not a yield-driven core rotation. (Full treatment in §3.)

## 3. QFIN — the crux (accounting confidence + sizing)

QFIN is the one name where the ranking hinges on judgment, not valuation. It has **three separable risks — fraud, credit cycle, and government policy — and they are different in kind.** Fraud and cash-repatriation are *permanent-loss* risks (you cannot wait them out). Credit cycle and policy are *survivable-drawdown* risks (a patient, drawdown-tolerant holder with no stop-loss can). Sizing should be governed by the permanent-loss legs; the drawdown legs are ones a long-horizon holder is structurally built to underwrite.

**Verified snapshot (mid-Jul 2026):** ~$13–14/ADS, near the 52-wk low (~$11–12), **down ~69% YoY** from $43.56; market cap ~$1.8B. **Trailing P/E ~2.0–2.7×** (FY2025 non-GAAP EPS US$6.69/ADS; GAAP NI RMB5.98B, −4.3%); **P/B ~0.86**; **dividend US$1.56/ADS/yr (semi-annual, raised ~8.6%) ≈ 11% yield**, ~23% of FY2025 non-GAAP EPS. BofA (May 2026) cut EPS ~55–57% and PT to $13.80 on "regulatory headwinds."

### Risk 1 — Fraud / accounting (permanent-loss). *Substantially de-risked, not eliminated.*
- Grizzly (Sep 2024) alleged >1,000× profit exaggeration vs SAMR filings; QFIN rebutted (GAAP-scope/VIE differences). Since then: **auditor Deloitte China (Big Four, PCAOB-inspectable) issued unqualified opinions on financials AND internal controls, no material weakness, no going-concern; FY2025 20-F filed; and NO SEC/PCAOB/CSRC action, NO auditor resignation, NO new class action, NO credible follow-up short.** The evidence has moved *against* Grizzly.
- **Ownership correction:** the real fundamental accumulator is **Triata** (Sean Ho; ~$60M, +188% in Q4 2025). **D. E. Shaw is quant** (weak signal). **"GreenWood Investors / Steven Wood" could not be confirmed to hold QFIN** and is likely conflated with **Greenwoods Asset Management (HK), which was *cutting* (~−21% Q4).** So the "smart money" leg rests mainly on Triata.
- **Residual permanent-loss tail:** the VIE/cash architecture — restricted onshore net assets ~RMB16.2B per FY2023 20-F, dividends funded offshore, repatriation subject to forex/WHT/capital controls — plus a **secondary (not dual-primary) HK listing (3660)** that only partially backstops a (currently low-probability) HFCAA delisting. Independent channel checks verify the *business is real*; they do less to verify *cash is there and mobile*. Net accounting confidence: **"opaque but probably genuine," not "high."**

### Risk 2 — Government policy (survivable-drawdown). *This is the "$12 question," and the vector is now specific.*
The angle is **not** Futu-style fund-flow/FX control, **not** an involution "price-cut" warning (credit is already rate-capped), and **not yet** a youth-debt borrower cap. It is a formalized **interest-rate-cap descent**, running *alongside* a pro-consumption push — and the structural reforms actually favor QFIN:
- **助贷新规 (loan-facilitation rules), effective 1 Oct 2025:** banks must white-list platform/guarantee partners; **all-in "comprehensive financing cost" (interest + guarantee + fees) capped at 24%**; fee-stacking (双融担) banned; platform "backstop" removed. **Effect = consolidation to compliant leaders** (Ant/JD/Tencent/Qifu). >5,600 non-compliant institutions and 400+ small-loan cos have exited since 2024; mid-tier rival Shuhe's valuation fell ~75%. **QFIN's Q4 2025 net profit reportedly exceeded all listed peers combined.** Regulation as a moat — matching the "banks rely on these platforms / govt won't kill consumption" intuition; the sector is becoming quasi-infrastructure for banks.
- **The real damage — rate cap grinding below 24% toward ~12% (4×LPR):** NFRA+PBOC 明示综合融资成本 rule (issued 15 Mar 2026, effective 1 Aug 2026) caps all-in cost at 24% across *all* licensed personal lenders and names 多头借贷/过度授信 as targets. Small-loan cos (247号, Dec 2025): stop new loans >24% now, cut >4×LPR share by end-2026, **all new loans ≤4×LPR (~12%) by end-2027.** Consumer-finance cos already held to ~20%. NFRA signaling extension of the "24%→~12% by 2027" path to the whole sector. **QFIN earns ~45% of revenue (~RMB8.6B) from its OWN network-microloan license at ~21–22% — this book is directly in the 12%-by-2027 crosshairs.** Its answer: shift to capital-light facilitation-for-banks (24%/20% caps) + overseas (Europe/LatAm/SE Asia).
- **Collections (催收):** national standard GB/T 45251-2025 (eff. 28 Feb 2025) — no calls 22:00–08:00, ≤3 calls/day, debtor-only — raises loss-given-default. **QFIN named in an NFRA 集中约谈 (13 Mar 2026)** over pricing/collection/marketing; a Fuzhou microcredit sub fined RMB1.19M (Dec 2025).
- **Counter-force (pro-consumption):** consumer-loan ceiling raised to RMB500k (+66%); fiscal interest subsidy (消费贷贴息, 1ppt) effective Sep 2025, extended to Dec 2026. Beijing pushes *volume up* while forcing *rates down* — net favors licensed, low-rate, prime-scale players; squeezes high-rate subprime.

### Risk 3 — Credit cycle (survivable-drawdown). *Materializing, possibly near peak.*
Q1 2026 net profit −47/−51% YoY; Q4 2025 Day-1 delinquency 6.1% (highest since Q2 2020), 30-day recovery 84.1% (lowest since Q2 2020), provisions +75% (receivables) / +1,115% (contingent). Leading indicators tentatively improving; management frames 90+ delinquency (3.5%) as lagging older vintages near peak. Recovery not yet in the numbers.

### "How bad could it get" — the $12 math
- **Trough earnings floor:** the rate-cap repricing of the ~45%-of-revenue microloan book toward ~12–15% plus a capital-light mix shift and normalized provisioning points to a **policy-compressed normalized EPS of ~$3–4/ADS** (roughly half of FY2025's $6.69). At $14 that is **~3.5–4.5× trough earnings** — i.e., **the current price already embeds a roughly-halved-and-stays-halved earnings outcome.**
- **Realistic non-fraud downside:** a worse-than-expected fast sector-wide 12% cap + continued credit deterioration keeps it at a distressed ~4–5× trough → ~**$8–9** (−35 to −40%). A true wipeout requires either fraud/cash-repatriation failure (Risk 1) or a low-probability P2P-style sector shutdown — which contradicts the pro-consumption push and bank reliance.
- **Recovery upside:** most-likely case (survivable compression + share gains, credit troughs 2026, buyback resumes) re-rating to 6–7× on stabilizing ~$4–5 EPS → **$25–35**, while paid ~11% to wait. Asymmetry ≈ **−35% bad-but-not-fraud vs +80–150% recovery, plus an 11% coupon** — provided Risk 1 holds.

### On your specific bull claims
- **"~4× even after a 50% earnings fall; ~11% yield, ~8% after a cut":** confirmed by the numbers. But the mirror image holds — a ~2× trailing P/E and 11% yield on a lender mid-credit-cycle *is* the market pricing the tail; the low multiple is simultaneously the margin of safety and the implied probability of impairment.
- **Buyback nuance:** the equity buyback is **paused** — QFIN retired ~$577M of converts in Q1 2026 instead, management citing an "unsettled macro and regulatory environment." Shareholder-friendly (kills 2030 dilution) but not fresh share shrinkage, and itself a tell on how management reads the policy risk.
- **AI credit-screening as free optionality:** agreed — take it as upside on the loss curve, don't pay for it.

### Sizing (US$6.6m → US$12–18m)
- **Yes, a modest increase is defensible.** The policy and credit legs are *survivable drawdown* — precisely the risk your long horizon + drawdown tolerance + no-stop-loss structure is built to underwrite. The fraud leg is now substantially de-risked (clean Deloitte ICFR, no escalation). What remains is the residual VIE/cash-repatriation tail — a genuine but lower permanent-loss risk.
- **Size to the ruin scenario, not the yield.** Set the position so a QFIN-specific permanent loss (−60% to −100% in the fraud/repatriation/sector-ban tail) costs no more than ~3–4% of NAV. Move to the **lower-to-middle of the range (~US$12–15M) now**; make the top end (US$18M) contingent on confirmation — a clean FY2025 20-F holds up, delinquency rolls over, and equity buyback resumes.
- **Fund from the excess USD, not by selling low-yielders.** Rotating quality ballast into the single highest-tail-risk name to lift portfolio income is reaching for yield and concentrating the exact risk you least want. Keep the low-yield quality as ballast; deploy fresh USD into QFIN.

### QFIN verdict
**High absolute upside, mid-tier risk-adjusted rank — a sized-for-ruin special situation that genuinely suits a patient, drawdown-tolerant holder.** The policy "$12 question" resolves favorably-enough: the vector is margin compression (survivable), not existence (a sector ban is low-probability), and the structural reforms hand QFIN share as rivals exit. The binding remaining risk is the VIE/cash tail plus a credit trough — hence *small*. Ranks below Genting, the carry names and Tencent/JD; above its cleaner twin PayPal on upside but below on certainty; above SMPL and CR Medical. Trigger to size *up* is a clean 20-F + delinquency rolling over + buyback resuming — not the 11% yield alone.

**All three still cover the 1.93% funding cost after a 30% distribution cut** — the income leg is very robust (~70% cut cushion). The danger is on the **capital and funding sides**:
- **−10% unit/share price ≈ −20% on 2× levered equity** — roughly two years of levered carry erased in one move, plus **margin-call risk**. This dominates the trade.
- **+100–200bp funding** compresses carry one-for-one; a rate rise *combined* with a 20–30% cut gets thin (e.g. Genting at 3.93% funding + 30% cut ≈ +0.6%).
- Digital Core's extra ~0.4–0.6pp headline spread is compensation for USD/SGD FX (SGD has structurally appreciated ~1–2%/yr vs USD), not free carry.

**Carry verdict:** CapitaLand Ascott is the best clean risk-adjusted SGD carry; Genting is comparable but with the least-covered dividend; keep leverage modest (the −10% capital move, not the dividend, is the real risk); CICT is the best "sleep-at-night" lower-yield carry.

## 6. Deploy-now vs keep-firepower

- **Keep meaningful USD firepower** for BABA / Baidu / PayPal / Flutter and a China-bank entry — most of these are "wait for price or a confirming catalyst," and holding cash has option value into a China-tech or credit dislocation.
- **But don't sit 100% in cash:** Tencent and JD are already worth starting, and the **SGD carry pays you to wait** (positive net carry that survives a 30% cut). A barbell — quality watchlist starters + positive-carry SGD income + a small QFIN special situation — beats an all-cash "wait."
- **Genting** is the anchor you already own: hold to the ~10% cap, trim on any results-driven rerating, and recycle proceeds into the watchlist/carry.
