**As of 14 July 2026.** Lens: distressed & recovery investing — permanent-capital-loss risk and accounting confidence weighted above optical cheapness.

This is analysis, not investment advice.

## 1. Headline verdict

1. **The best risk-adjusted deployments right now are the higher-quality watchlist names and the SGD carry — not QFIN and not most of the "deployment candidates."** Specifically: **Tencent** and **JD.com** (buy-on-weakness quality/value), the **CapitaLand Ascott Trust / Genting SGD carry**, and **China Unicom** (improving-FCF income) screen ahead of QFIN, CR Medical, Haidilao, SMPL and PayPal on a permanent-loss-adjusted basis.
2. **"Keep firepower and wait" is broadly the right instinct — but not for everything.** BABA, Baidu, PayPal, Flutter and the two China banks are genuinely "wait for a better price / a confirming catalyst." But **Tencent and JD are already at levels worth starting**, and the **carry trade is a positive-carry way to be paid while you wait.**
3. **Genting: trim discipline, not add.** You already hold a large winning position. The balance sheet (net cash, sub-book) makes it the lowest-permanent-loss carry base, but the dividend is not covered by earnings and the RWS 2.0 payoff is 2028–2030. Take it to your ~10% cap on strength; keep it, don't grow it beyond the cap.
4. **QFIN is a high-variance *special situation*, not a core holding.** It has the highest absolute upside among the equities if the accounts are clean, but a bimodal outcome (VIE/ADR + still-deteriorating credit). The smart-money ownership and extreme valuation justify a *sized-for-ruin* position, not a yield-driven core rotation. (Full treatment in §3.)

## 3. QFIN — the crux (accounting confidence + sizing)

QFIN is the one name where the ranking hinges on a judgment, not a valuation. Treat it as **two separate questions** — because the bull points address the first and barely touch the second.

### Question 1 — Are the accounts real?
- **The allegation:** Grizzly Research (Sept 2024) claimed QFIN exaggerated SEC-reported net income by >1,000× vs its SAMR/SAIC entity filings — "fake financials," related-party manipulation via Shanghai Qibutianxia, chairman Hongyi Zhou a "bad actor." QFIN rebutted forcefully (27 Sep 2024), arguing Grizzly misread SAMR filings and that its main China entities reported PRC-GAAP net profit *higher* than consolidated US-GAAP (PRC-vs-US GAAP + VIE-scope differences). One of QFIN's specific rebuttal sub-claims (the cash-decline bridge) did **not** hold up under adversarial checking — a yellow flag on the rebuttal's completeness, not proof of fraud.
- **What the bull points genuinely improve:** Respected fundamental holders (Greenwood — Steven Wood, long concentrated-value record; **Triata** — a China-focused fund that does independent channel verification of loan volumes, app data and funding-partner relationships) provide real, if indirect, comfort that the **business and revenue are real**. Independent verification of operational reality is the strongest available rebuttal to the "fake business / fake revenue" half of Grizzly. (D. E. Shaw "adding" is a weak *fundamental* signal — a quant multi-strat position can be factor/convert-arb/stat-arb driven; don't lean on it as an endorsement.)
- **What they do *not* resolve:** Third-party verification is good at "do the loans/borrowers exist"; it is far weaker at "**is the reported cash actually there and repatriable, and are provisions adequate through this credit cycle.**" Restricted cash, cash held at onshore VIE vs the offshore listco, and provision adequacy sit largely behind the same audited/regulatory data the shorts dispute. So the ownership evidence lowers *fake-business* probability more than *cash-isn't-there / under-provisioned* probability. **Accounting confidence: moves from "opaque" toward "opaque but probably genuine business," not to "high."**

### Question 2 — Is the business recovering? (independent of fraud)
- **No — not yet.** Q1 2026: net profit **−51% YoY to RMB880m**, revenue **−16.7%**, 90+ day delinquency **3.5%** (up from 2.71% end-2025 and 2.09% end-2024). Net margin fell ~38% → ~22.5%.
- **Tentative green shoots:** leading indicators improved (Day-1 delinquency 5.7% vs 6.1%; 30-day collection 85.8% vs 84.1%), and management frames the 90+ rise as a lagging echo of older vintages possibly near peak. But the *recovery is not yet in the numbers*, and a lending book's optical cheapness is worth little if charge-offs are still climbing.

### On the specific bull claims
- **"~4× even if earnings fall 50%; ~11% yield, ~8% after a 20–30% cut."** Directionally fair — the optical cheapness and cash return are extreme and real *if the accounts and cash are genuine*. But note the mirror image: a ~2–4× P/E and ~11% cash yield on a *lender in a deteriorating credit cycle* is not a free lunch — it **is the market's probability-weighting of the tail**. The same low multiple is the bull's margin of safety and the bear's implied ~probability of impairment. Both readings are true; which dominates depends entirely on Question 1's cash/provision leg.
- **The dividend is funded from VIE/onshore cash** that must flow up through the offshore structure — the very cash pathway the shorts question. A high covered-looking yield and repatriation risk are not independent; treat "8% even after a cut" as *conditional* on the cash being real and mobile.
- **AI credit-screening as free optionality:** agreed — don't pay for it; take it as upside if it bends the loss curve.

### Sizing (your US$6.6m → US$12–18m question)
- **Direction: yes, a modest increase is defensible** — the mitigants (smart-money verification, extreme valuation, large capital return) justify moving QFIN up from "avoid/token" to a **genuine but sized-for-ruin special situation.** It is *not* a core holding, because the binding tail (VIE/ADR structure + a still-rising loss cycle + un-verifiable cash/provisions) can produce a permanent 60–100% loss that no amount of desktop diligence fully closes.
- **Size to the ruin scenario, not the yield.** Pick the position so a QFIN-specific wipeout (assume −60% to −100%) costs no more than ~2–4% of total NAV. Move to the **lower-to-middle of your range (≈US$12–15m) now**, and make the top end (US$18m) *contingent* on accounting-confidence confirmation: a clean FY2025 20-F + auditor sign-off, continued buyback execution, and delinquency actually rolling over.
- **Fund it from the excess USD — not by selling low-yielders to raise income.** Rotating quality ballast into the single highest-tail-risk name to lift portfolio yield is reaching for yield and concentrating the exact risk you least want. Keep the low-yield quality as portfolio insurance; deploy fresh capital into QFIN.

### QFIN verdict
**Rank: lower-middle of the equity book — high absolute upside, low risk-adjusted rank.** Below Genting, the carry names, Tencent/JD and its cleaner deep-value twin PayPal (same "cheap + buyback" DNA without the fraud/VIE tail); roughly alongside SMPL on permanent-loss but well above it on upside; above CR Medical. Own it small as a special situation, funded with fresh USD, sized so the bad tail can't hurt the portfolio — and let a clean 20-F / falling delinquency, not the 11% yield, be the trigger to size up.

## 5. SGD leveraged carry (funding 1.93%)

Singapore has no dividend withholding tax; S-REIT distributions to individuals are tax-exempt; Genting dividends are one-tier tax-exempt → withholding drag ≈ 0 for the two SGD names. Digital Core distributes **USD** (FX mismatch vs SGD borrowing) and has US tax leakage netted into its DPU.

**Base carry**

| Name | Ccy | Yield | − 1.93% funding | Net carry | FX mismatch? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genting Singapore | SGD | 6.50% | 1.93% | **+4.57%** | No |
| CapitaLand Ascott Trust | SGD | 6.70% | 1.93% | **+4.77%** | No |
| Digital Core REIT | USD | 7.10% | 1.93% | **+5.17% (pre-FX)** | **Yes** |

**Stress test — net carry after a distribution cut** (yield × (1−cut) − 1.93%)

| Cut | Genting | CLAS | Digital Core |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | +4.57% | +4.77% | +5.17% |
| −10% | +3.92% | +4.10% | +4.46% |
| −20% | +3.27% | +3.43% | +3.75% |
| −30% | +2.62% | +2.76% | +3.04% |
| Breakeven cut | ~70% | ~71% | ~73% |

**All three still cover the 1.93% funding cost after a 30% distribution cut** — the income leg is very robust (~70% cut cushion). The danger is on the **capital and funding sides**:
- **−10% unit/share price ≈ −20% on 2× levered equity** — roughly two years of levered carry erased in one move, plus **margin-call risk**. This dominates the trade.
- **+100–200bp funding** compresses carry one-for-one; a rate rise *combined* with a 20–30% cut gets thin (e.g. Genting at 3.93% funding + 30% cut ≈ +0.6%).
- Digital Core's extra ~0.4–0.6pp headline spread is compensation for USD/SGD FX (SGD has structurally appreciated ~1–2%/yr vs USD), not free carry.

**Carry verdict:** CapitaLand Ascott is the best clean risk-adjusted SGD carry; Genting is comparable but with the least-covered dividend; keep leverage modest (the −10% capital move, not the dividend, is the real risk); CICT is the best "sleep-at-night" lower-yield carry.

## 6. Deploy-now vs keep-firepower

- **Keep meaningful USD firepower** for BABA / Baidu / PayPal / Flutter and a China-bank entry — most of these are "wait for price or a confirming catalyst," and holding cash has option value into a China-tech or credit dislocation.
- **But don't sit 100% in cash:** Tencent and JD are already worth starting, and the **SGD carry pays you to wait** (positive net carry that survives a 30% cut). A barbell — quality watchlist starters + positive-carry SGD income + a small QFIN special situation — beats an all-cash "wait."
- **Genting** is the anchor you already own: hold to the ~10% cap, trim on any results-driven rerating, and recycle proceeds into the watchlist/carry.
